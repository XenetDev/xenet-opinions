# Public Reaction to the 20% Price Increase

This report summarises how simulated customers, competitors and commentators responded across X.com and Reddit.com after the announcement of a 20% increase in the monthly subscription price.

## Overall sentiment

The first rounds were dominated by frustration. Roughly two thirds of early posts criticised the timing of the change, and the phrase "no warning" appeared in many of them.

By the midpoint sentiment had stabilised. Long-standing customers argued that the product still compared well on value, and several of them publicly defended the change.

- Negative sentiment peaked in round 4 and declined steadily afterwards
- Neutral and mixed posts made up the largest share by the final round
- Positive posts were rare but received the most engagement

## Churn risk by segment

Small teams and individual users were the most price sensitive. They were also the most likely to name a specific competitor and to share migration guides.

Mid-size teams focused on contract terms. Many asked whether existing annual plans would be honoured, and the answer shaped their tone more than the increase itself.

Large accounts rarely mentioned price. Their posts centred on support quality and roadmap commitments, which suggests low short-term churn risk.

## Competitor and media response

Competitors moved quickly. Two of them posted limited-time discounts aimed at switching customers within the first three rounds.

Industry commentators framed the increase as part of a wider trend. Their coverage generally softened the tone of the discussion among undecided users.

## Conclusion

The price increase is likely to be absorbed by most of the customer base, provided the rollout is communicated clearly and existing annual plans are protected.

The main exposure is among small teams, where a targeted grandfathering offer or a lower-priced tier would reduce the risk of switching. Monitoring competitor promotions during the first month is recommended.

Recommended actions:

- Announce the change with at least 30 days of notice
- Confirm that annual plans keep their current price until renewal
- Offer small teams a discounted tier before competitor promotions land
